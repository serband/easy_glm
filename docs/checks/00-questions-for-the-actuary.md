# Questions for the actuary (from the 0.4 plan review)

Each question has the default we are building with. Answer any of them at any
time; the defaults are parameters, not architecture, so changing an answer later
is cheap.

| # | Question | Default we are using | Actuary's answer (2026-09-03) | Effect |
|---|----------|----------------------|-------------------------------|--------|
| Q1 | For a piecewise-linear factor (e.g. mileage), beyond the smallest and largest values seen in training: should the curve stay **flat**, or keep its last slope? | **Flat** (clamped at the training range). The clamp points are shown in the table. | Flat beyond the training range: if the highest mileage seen is 30,000 and its relativity is 1.30, everything above 30,000 stays at 1.30. | Confirms the default. |
| Q2 | Where should relativity 1.00 sit on a continuous curve? | At the lower knot of the band with the most exposure, so it is a round, visible number. | 1.00 where there is the most exposure, not at the smallest or largest value. | Confirms the default (lower knot of the most-exposed band). **Recorded for `continuous` terms (B2):** a single band has only two points a rate table can carry 1.00 at, so it sits at the lower clamp unless the exposure-weighted median is past 60 % of the way up the range (off centre on purpose: a halfway split would let a mid-range factor flip between the two ends between refits on sampling noise). With one slope that choice only rescales the base rate, never the ratios between relativities; `base="reference"` and the base-rate override move it. |
| Q3 | Should the lasso prefer curves with few **bends** (long straight sloped sections) or few **slopes** (flat wherever the data does not insist)? | Few bends (the AGLM hinge basis). Monotone constraints are therefore not offered on linear terms in 0.4. | Bias towards fewer **slopes**, i.e. more flat sections. Non-monotonic shapes can signal a missed interaction; each factor should stay intuitively interpretable. | **Built (piece B2).** The linear basis penalises the slope of each band — one fitted number per band, driven to exactly zero unless the data insists — instead of the bends; each band pays the same penalty for the same *rise* in relativity, whatever its width and however few policies reach it (before this, a wide thin tail band bought its rise for ~4 % of what the body of the curve paid); monotone constraints are sign bounds on those slopes and are available on linear terms again. See `docs/checks/b-linear.md`. |
| Q4 | Minimum exposure for an interaction cell to get its own adjustment? | 0.5% of training exposure per interaction, editable. Cells below it show 1.00 with their exposure alongside. | Fine. | Confirms the default. |
| Q5 | When `A × B` is added, the main-effect tables for A and B change (the split between mains and cells is not unique). Acceptable, or should mains be frozen and the interaction fitted as a second stage? | Joint fit; the before/after main tables are shown in the actuarial check. | Mains are frozen. Interactions are fitted only after offsetting the main effects: a two-stage process — finalise the mains, then find and fit interactions as stand-alone adjustments on top of stage 1. | **Built (piece A2).** A model with interactions is fitted in two stages: stage 1 is the main-effect model — the same fit, number for number, that the model gets with no interaction — and stage 2 fits the cells on top of it with stage 1's linear predictor as the offset and no intercept of its own. Main tables and the base rate are therefore identical with and without the interaction (shown in `docs/checks/a-interactions.md`: every band 0.00% against up to −21.1% under the old joint fit), and a cell is a pure adjustment. The price, on the same data: holdout Gini 0.3083 rather than the joint fit's 0.3105 (0.3072 with no interaction). |
| Q6 | Rate-change setup (offset = log of current premium): should the export read as "multiplier on current premium" (base rate = overall change, relativities = differential changes)? | Yes. | Yes. | **Built (piece E1).** A `current_premium` role; `log(<premium>)` is derived by the data steps and pre-filled as the offset of every new model; the Rate tables page, the Export page and the Excel `Summary` sheet all label the numbers "multiplier on current premium" and say that the base rate carries the level and each relativity is a differential change. One correction to the wording of the question: the base rate is the change for the **base risk** (relativity 1.00 on every factor), which is not the same number as the book's overall move — see question 2 of `docs/checks/e-f-extras-cli.md`. |
| Q7 | Binomial (e.g. lapse) models: export odds relativities with a label, or probabilities by band? | Odds relativities; the scorer returns probabilities. | (no comment yet) | **Built (piece E3) on the default.** The tables multiply the odds and are labelled "odds relativity" on every page and in Excel; the base rate is the base risk's odds; `RateModel.predict` returns a probability in (0, 1) and **refuses** to be multiplied by an exposure column. Changing the answer would change labels and a scorer, not the architecture. |
| Q8 | For frequency models, actual = Σ claims / Σ exposure and expected = Σ fitted claims / Σ exposure — confirm. | Yes; the rate/count flag is stored in the model file so the editor stops guessing. | (no comment yet) | default stands |
| Q9 | Should any variable *default* to piecewise-linear rather than step? | No: every numeric variable defaults to step; individual variables (typically mileage, sums insured, vehicle value) are switched to linear on the Design page. | All numeric variables are treated as step unless explicitly specified as not needing knots. | Confirms the default; **built (piece B2)**. Explicit overrides per variable on the Design page: `categorical` (each value a level), `linear` (piecewise, with knots) and `continuous` (a single slope on the raw value, no knots — a linear term with one band, so it shares the rate table, editor, Excel sheet and exported script). |
| Q10 | For a piecewise-linear factor, should the default **upper clamp** be the training maximum (the curve follows the data through thin tails) or a high quantile such as the 99.5th percentile (thin tails pooled flat, as a step design would)? | Q11 | Compare page: what tolerance should list a band as "changed"? | 1% on the log scale (a box on the page; a wrong default costs one keystroke). (no comment yet) | default stands |
